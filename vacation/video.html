Why Hawaii?

Hawaii is the only U.S. state located entirely in the tropics, and it is the only state composed entirely of islands. This unique geography has given rise to some of the most diverse ecosystems on the planet — from the tropical beaches of Oahu to the alpine deserts atop Mauna Kea on the Big Island. Hawaii contains 8 of the world's 13 climate zones, making it one of the most ecologically diverse places on Earth.

Hawaii's cultural richness is equally remarkable. Native Hawaiian traditions — hula, surfing, canoe voyaging, and the aloha spirit itself — are actively practiced and celebrated throughout the islands. Visitors are welcomed into a culture that values connection, respect for the land, and joy in community.

Best Times to Visit

Hawaii's tropical climate makes it a year-round destination. Temperatures typically range between 75°F and 85°F on the coasts, with slightly cooler temperatures in the mountains. The dry season runs from April through October and is ideal for beach activities, hiking, and water sports. The wet season from November through March brings occasional rain showers, but they are typically brief and followed by spectacular rainbows. December through April is also prime whale watching season, when humpback whales migrate to Hawaiian waters to breed and calve.
